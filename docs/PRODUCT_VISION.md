# Product Vision: A Library for Faithful Families

## In One Sentence

> FamilyPath is a library that helps Christian families learn together—through hymns, books, activities, and daily rhythms—with a powerful engine hidden beneath a simple, warm interface.

---

## The Core Metaphor

**A vintage car with a beast of an engine.**

From the outside: simple, beautiful, inviting. A library you want to browse. Hymns you want to play. Books you want to read to your children.

Under the hood: a sophisticated engine capable of generating age-appropriate daily suggestions, tracking growth across years, and helping parents form their children with intention.

The engine stays quiet until you need it.

---

## What This App Is

### It Is a Library

- 100+ hymns to sing and memorize
- 100+ picture books across history, faith, and formation
- Westminster Shorter Catechism with age-appropriate versions
- Memory verses organized for systematic learning
- Developmental activities for little ones
- History courses from picture books to full narratives

### It Is a Daily Rhythm Tool

For families who want structure:
- Subscribe to Learning Paths (Hymn Journey, Catechism, African History, etc.)
- See a combined daily rhythm: "Today: Hymn → Verse → Book → Activity"
- Track completion toward meaningful goals
- Never feel overwhelmed—the rhythm fits your time

### It Is a Growth Companion

The app quietly tracks what your family has done:
- "You've learned 42 of 100 hymns"
- "Your children have completed 23 history books"
- "After memorizing 50 verses, here's the ground you've covered..."

It never judges. It celebrates completion.

---

## What This App Is NOT

### It Is Not a School

There are no grades. No transcripts. No credits. No graduation requirements.

Learning happens through living—reading together, singing together, discussing together. The app supports this. It doesn't systematize it into a school.

### It Is Not a Tutor

AI helps parents—explains activities, suggests books, summarizes progress. But AI never teaches children directly (unless parent explicitly enables it for older children, with full visibility).

### It Is Not Gamified

No streaks. No points. No leaderboards. No dopamine loops.

Completion is its own reward: "We finished the African History picture books together."

### It Is Not Overwhelming

The home screen is calm. A library to browse. A daily rhythm if you want one. Nothing demands attention.

---

## The Two Modes

### Passive Mode (Default)

Open the app. Browse. Play hymns while cooking. Read a bedtime story. Mark it done if you want.

No pressure. No structure required. Just a library.

**Who it's for:**
- Working fathers wanting 15 minutes of intentional time
- Families who have their own curriculum but want hymns and books
- Anyone who just wants to listen to hymns or read stories

### Active Mode (Opt-In)

Subscribe to Learning Paths. Get a daily rhythm. Track progress toward completing the library.

**Who it's for:**
- Parents systematically forming toddlers
- Families wanting to learn 100+ hymns over time
- Homeschoolers using this as their liturgy/history backbone

## The Learning Paths

Paths are curated journeys through the library:

| Path | What It Covers | Outcome |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **Hymn Journey** | 100+ hymns in thoughtful order | "We've learned the great hymns of the faith" |
| **Catechism Path** | WSC Q1-107, weekly | "Our children know the foundations of Reformed theology" |
| **Family Liturgy** | Daily hymn + verse + catechism | "We start each day grounded in truth" |
| **African History (Young)** | Picture book series | "Our children know their African heritage" |
| **African History (Full)** | Textbook chapters | "Our teens understand African history deeply" |
| **Toddler Development** | Age-appropriate activities | "We've built a foundation of skills through play" |
| **Pastor Curtis Series** | His books in order | "We've read the wisdom of a faithful pastor" |

Families can subscribe to multiple paths. The engine combines them into one daily rhythm.

## The Beast Engine

Hidden beneath the simple interface is a powerful engine:

### What It Can Do

1. **Age-Specific Suggestions**
   - Family has a 5-month-old and a 3-year-old? Different activities for each.
   - Tiered expectations: Tier 1 (youngest), Tier 2 (middle), Tier 3 (oldest)

2. **Domain Balancing**
   - Motor, cognitive, language, social-emotional coverage
   - Biblical domains: wisdom, stature, favor with God, favor with man

3. **Child Profiles**
   - What each child has completed
   - Strengths and interests noted over time
   - Growth summaries: "Here's how Noah has developed this year"

4. **Time-Aware Planning**
   - Parent says "We have 20 minutes today"
   - Engine picks the highest-priority items that fit

5. **Library Completion Tracking**
   - "You've finished 100% of the hymns"
   - "Here's what your children have gained from this journey"

### When It Activates

The engine is **dormant by default**. It wakes when:
- You subscribe to paths that need scheduling
- You enable "Smart Suggestions"
- You have multiple children with different ages

For families who just want to browse—the engine stays asleep.

No to-do list. No pressure. Just a library.

## What the Parent Feels

### They Feel:

- **Supported** — explanations available, not imposed
- **Free** — nothing breaks if a day is skipped
- **Oriented** — today fits into a long-term arc
- **Capable** — "I can do this with my family"

### They Don't Feel:

- Judged
- Behind
- Overwhelmed
- Managed by software

---

## What the Child Experiences

### Ages 0-6

The child barely notices the app. They experience:
- Stories read by parents
- Songs sung together
- Activities done with mom or dad
- Presence, not screens

### Ages 7-12

The child may see a simple view:
- "Read Chapter 3 of African History"
- "Practice your catechism question"
- Mark complete when done

Parent sees everything they do.

### Ages 13+

The teen uses the family account to:
- Read History course independently
- Study Catechism/Apologetics
- Work through Pastor Curtis books
- Build their own reading habits

Parent retains visibility and control.

## The Library as End Goal

### Completion Matters

The ultimate goal is not "graduation." It's completing the library:

- "We've sung all 100 hymns together"
- "Our children know the Westminster Shorter Catechism"
- "We've read every African History book"
- "We've done 200 developmental activities"

These completions represent real formation. Not credentials—transformation.

### What Completion Means

After completing the Hymn Journey:
> "Your family now knows the great hymns of the faith—songs that have sustained Christians for centuries. Your children carry these melodies and truths with them."

After completing African History:
> "Your children understand their heritage. They know the stories of faith, courage, and wisdom from the African continent."

## The Future (Not Now)

If FamilyPath succeeds as a library, it could eventually grow into a full homeschool solution with:

- Math and Science curricula
- Language Arts
- Apprenticeship tracking
- Transcripts and portfolios

**But not now.** Now we build the library. Now we perfect the paths. Now we make the daily rhythm beautiful.

The full school comes later—when families love the simple version so much they ask for more.

---

## The Final Measure of Success

The app has succeeded when:

1. A family completes the hymn library and their children carry those songs forever
2. A parent says "I actually enjoy our morning rhythm now"
3. A teenager knows African History better than most adults
4. A father with 15 minutes feels like he spent meaningful time with his kids
5. Families say: "We don't need this like we used to—and that's a good thing"

> **The best version of this app eventually makes itself less necessary.**

That is the desired end.
