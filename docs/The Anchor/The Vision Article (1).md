# **What Is Wrong with the Ugandan Education System**

## **The Fruit of the System**

The National Planning Authority reports that nearly 87% of Ugandan graduates are not "job-ready." We have normalized a culture where university degree holders ride *boda bodas* not out of an entrepreneurial spirit, but out of desperation. We are producing "theoretical adults"—loaded with definitions but stripped of the skills needed to build families and economies.

I wish it was just a problem of employment and skill we were talking about, but no. Immorality and laziness is at an all-time high. The same people that look for work get and lack the wisdom and character to keep it. Our leaders have failed to champion the nation out of its economic and developmental plight, because of greed. 

Jesus gave us a simple metric for evaluating any institution: "A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a bad tree bear good fruit." (Matthew 7:18).

For decades, Ugandan parents have planted their children in the soil of the modern school system, watering it with exorbitant fees and sacrifice. They were promised a harvest of disciplined, capable, and employable adults, but all have ripped is dust.

## **What Is Education?**

### **The Surface and the Root**

Whenever an analysis is conducted, we usually circle the usual suspects: outdated curricula, theoretical teaching, underpaid staff, and lack of resources. While true, these are surface wounds. The root issue is deeper.

Historically, "education" was not the same as "schooling." To educate was to raise a child—shaping habits, character, and faith for responsibility in the family, the church, and the world. To be educated was not to be crowned with every other D; the Diplomas and Degrees and PhDs.

Over time, we narrowed the definition. Education became classroom instruction, exams, and certificates. Schooling replaced education, but nothing replaced the work of moral and spiritual formation.

### **A Webster Definition**

Noah Webster’s 1828 dictionary captures the original intent:

“The bringing up, as of a child... intended to enlighten the understanding, correct the temper, and form the manners and habits of youth, and fit them for usefulness in their future stations.”

This definition refuses the notion that to prepare a child for the future is to just give them head knowledge. Webster includes instruction, but he refuses to separate it from upbringing and character development. Education, in his view, moves a person from childhood to responsible adulthood. It addresses the temper, the behaviors, the temperament and disposition. It is a comprehensive attempt at training for independent adulthood.

### **The Myth of Neutrality**

Why has a shift been widely accepted from focusing on the aspects of Education to just a few? Robert L. Dabne, argued that education can never be neutral. To teach values, discipline, and judgment is to take a stand on what is right, good, and true; very widely disputed issues. This simple stand provides a two-fold verdict on the Ugandan Education system.

Moral formation always rests on religious foundations, whether they are acknowledged or not. When a system claims to educate while refusing to name its moral and spiritual commitments, it does not become neutral—it by default ascribes to godlessness and secularism. This is the first snare the Ugandan Education system is stuck in. 

In an attempt to stay quiet about matters of religion to accommodate everyone, we have lost it. We have a “national” curriculum body, and a “national” examination board, that both silently claim that they’ll focus on knowledge and skills, and leave questions of character and faith to families, and religious entities. Is this separation practical? 

Not only is there little to no time for parents to carry out their gapping section of the task, it is also wrong to assume that there is no counteractive and biasing engagement when it comes to formation that is happening in the schools. Are Schools fairly and squarely staying in their box and sticking to Math and Science only? How do you teach and never once, for the 10+ years that they spend in the schools, form the character and disposition of the child.  

Education is not either Formation or Knowledge, and nor is it both in just any order. Education is both formation and knowledge in that order. The acquisition of which demands and implies a simultaneous unity, under caring but methodical hands. This can not be affordable to the Ugandan school environment that is far too greedy, and far too immoral, fully bent on exploitation of the Ugandan parent. If giving head knowledge takes higher precedence, then formation is going to happen haphazardly and unmonitored. 

Until we recover a clear sense of education as first, the formation of the child—thier morals, disposition, temperament, and faith—Uganda’s education debates will continue to circle the surface, unable to name the true weight of the problem.

## **Order Without God Is Still Disorder**

## **Sin vs. Structure**

As we step further into analyzing a secular system, one might ask, “But it is secular; how do you expect any good to come from it?” But is the system failing simply because man is sinful? While this is a genuine question, I refuse to just simply wave the Total Depravity card, and I beg us to think more deeply on the matter.

Think about it this way. In a home where the children are unregenerate, Christian parents, thinking biblically, will still hold high moral expectations on their unbelieving children. No parent can excuse himself by saying the reason there is disorder in his home is that all their children were born sinful. Like wise, the state has laws. And these laws are enforced on the general population, believing and unbelievers alike. 

While the human heart is depraved, God has ordained structures—family authority, discipline, and law—to restrain sin and maintain order in his common grace. Even fallen societies can produce disciplined workers, and great economies, if they respect these structures.

The Ugandan system fails not just because it is run by and for sinners. It fails because it has abandoned the God-ordained structures that restrain chaos. We have handed formation over to institutions never designed to carry it.

### **The Catastrophic Classroom**

The failure is visible in our use of time. Authority follows time. While parents and churches get the margins, schools claim the child’s most alert, formative hours. Fathers, nurture them\! This is the biblical mandate to every parent and it goes so far as to define both the scope of this process and the time at which it must happen. But let's focus on the time to stay true to the scope of this discussion. 

Our education system has not only failed to name its god, it has also taken the lion's share of the child’s time. We have so far developed that Education has to do with teaching head knowledge too, and it’s true that teaching Calculus and Sentence Diagramming is complicated; but isn’t 10 hours a day is an overkill? 

If it were these long, long hours with a better and more natural setting, I would refrain. But we take groups of children—large herds of the same age and immaturity—and we isolate them from the rest of the world and all adult wisdom, from 8am to 6pm. We leave them with their “caring teacher”. One teacher can not form 50-200 children, let alone teach them math and science; they can only manage their behavior, so that in pain they grade their papers late at night. In this vacuum, peer culture rules. Status is gained through rebellion and humor, and the wise is the notorious who is never caught.

Some parents hope their children will be "missionaries," and “ambassadors for Christ”, in these environments. That their children will be good examples and shine the light in the darkness. Be warned, dear parents. These environments wield the highest formation power over a rising generation than years of counseling. Be not deceived. Bad company corrupts good morals.

### **Why "Christian" Schools like wise Fail**

The vast majority of “christian” schools still subscribe to the National secular curriculum, and pledge their allegiance to its “neutral” examination board. All that changes is that there is a chapel service here and there; and perhaps, a prayer is said right before evolution is taught as a fact. Besides this, the structure remains misordered, and set against that which God describes. Time is still institutionalized and it therefore follows that formative authority is still outsourced.

The graduate of a system as such is usually fluent in religious language, and seemingly expressive of saving faith, but almost always lacking the discipline and endurance required to live out the faith they profess. You find “church boys” in their thirties refusing any form of responsibility and commitment to family leadership. This is not a failure of Christianity, but of a structure designed to mass-produce employees rather than raise sons and daughters in obedience to God.

The system is not evil by intention, but by design, and so any any diagnosis prescribed must point to the decaying core. Misordering what God has clearly designed in his revealed word, always produces broken outcomes.

## **The Fear of the Lord Is the Beginning of Wisdom**

### **Time Reveals Loyalty**

Every day, each parent answers the question: *What deserves the best of my child’s time?* The tug-of-war is between knowledge and skills verses faith and character formation. Prioritizing schooling over formation is a confession of loyalty.

Ugandan parents, more than anyone else, understand that knowledge and skills matter. Children must learn to read, calculate, work, and think. Hunger and poverty are real. The job hunt will eat the shoe down if you hold no certification, and yet getting one literally leaves parents no choice but to send their children to the school. 

But one truth cannot be avoided. When moral and spiritual formation are consistently given less time than schooling, a decision has already been made. Character has been ranked below competence. Systems have been trusted more than Scripture. Outcomes have been valued above obedience. Children are being prepared to survive the world rather than to fear the God who made it.

This is not an insult, but a call to clarity. If we believe this ordering is wrong, a different life is required—one of sacrifice, reordering finances, and accepting that responsibility cannot be delegated.

### **No Option for "Preference"**

God did not leave child formation to personal preference. One of the most dangerous assumptions a parent could make is that God left the question of child formation open to personal preference or cultural convenience. That this depends on the nature of your work schedule or the special circumstances in which your family is set. Scripture makes no such allowance.

In Deuteronomy 6, God explicitly commands parents to shape their children with His words continually, woven into daily life—when sitting, when walking, when lying down, and when rising. Formation is presented not as a program, but as a way of living.

In Ephesians 5 and 6, this responsibility is sharpened further. Husbands and wives are addressed first and commanded to live a certain way and if this is done in the presence of their children it is forever nailed in their consciences. Furthermore, fathers are explicitly addressed and held accountable for the nature and upbringing of their children. Children are to be brought up, not handed over. They are to be disciplined and instructed in the Lord, not merely supervised, enrolled, or managed.

Scripture is striking for what it never does. It never assigns this task to schools. It never treats institutions as moral guardians. It never excuses parents because the times are hard nor does it allow compromise in the case of tough economies. 

Scripture never assigns this task to the church or the government. Parents will stand before God—not with the degrees they gave their children in the face of this issue, but with the souls entrusted to them, and what stat they'll be in. This truth is meant to awaken those who have quietly surrendered their calling.

### **3.3 Wisdom First, Then Labor**

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge. This is not a cliché; it is the basis for all how seek to educate.

Holistic education builds on this bedrock. It does not excuse laziness but rather provides a foundation for all the muscle we place into the education of our children. The biblical path is narrower. It looks like fathers training sons to govern themselves for the day they'll step out of the home. It looks like mothers forming daughters in wisdom to become responsible and God fearing  mothers and wives. It involves working harder than everyone else and rejecting entertainment as a lifestyle.

Will my child learn math and science?? Will they learn how to write well and discuss big ideas in front of a multitude of people?? Will they grow up to life a fruitful and successful life?? While the schools give no such assurance either I'll say this, obedience must come first then we must labor to teach them everything else in our power to teach them. We must trust that in obeying God is where true blessedness will rise. We must fear him because it is to him that we will give account. 

The hope for Uganda does not begin with better schools, but with parents who fear God more than they fear poverty and the uncertainty of the future.

## **4\. Conclusion**

### **The Parent’s Verdict**

The Ugandan education system is structurally incapable of fulfilling the biblical mandate to educate the rising generation. It attempts to sever the mind from the soul, inevitably causing an epidemic of godlessness and immorality.

We must choose this day whom we will serve. We cannot be wise in our own eyes; we must trust God’s ordering of the family above the state’s ordering of the school. We cannot rest until our children are fully formed—not just as workers for the economy, but as servants of the Most High.
