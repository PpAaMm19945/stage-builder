## 1. The Liturgy of Life

Education is not just a transfer of information; it is a formation of loves through habit and worship. As James K.A. Smith notes, "We are what we love."

> "You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise." (Deuteronomy 6:7)

Our "liturgy" is the pattern of our days. We aim to structure time so that God is the reference point for all activity.

**Summary:** We structure the day around worship and prayer to form the child's heart, not just fill their mind.

## 2. Daily Rhythms

A sample rhythm for the homeschooling day:

### Morning (Rise)
-   **Opening:** Family prayer and singing (Doxology).
-   **Scripture:** Reading a short passage.
-   **Recitation:** Reviewing the weekly memory verse.

### Mid-Day (Sit & Walk)
-   **Instruction:** Core subjects (Wisdom & Order).
-   **Work:** Chores or handicraft (Stature).
-   **Rest:** Quiet reading or nap.

### Evening (Lie Down)
-   **Family Worship:** Singing, Catechism Q&A, Prayer.
-   **Blessing:** Pronouncing a benediction over the children.

**Summary:** The day is bookended by worship, sanctifying the time in between.

## 3. Catechism Progression

We recommend a systematic approach to catechesis, adapting to the child's stage.

| Stage | Resource | Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Years (0-5)** | *Catechism for Young Children* / *First Catechism* | Simple Q&A about God, Creation, and Sin. |
| **Lower Primary (6-9)** | *Westminster Shorter Catechism* (Q1-38) / *Heidelberg* (Lord's Day 1-20) | Memorizing key definitions verbatim. |
| **Middle School (10-13)** | *Westminster Shorter Catechism* (Complete) / *Heidelberg* (Complete) | Understanding the system of doctrine. |
| **Upper School (14-18)** | *Westminster Confession of Faith* / *Canons of Dort* | Deep study of proofs and historical context. |

**Summary:** Catechesis moves from simple truths to robust theological systems as the child matures.

## 4. Scripture Memory System

We use a "review and retain" system to ensure long-term retention.

-   **Daily Box:** The current verse being learned.
-   **Odd/Even Days:** Verses mastered recently.
-   **Day of Week:** Verses mastered longer ago (Monday = Ps 23, Tuesday = Beatitudes).
-   **Day of Month:** Old verses kept fresh.

**Core Passages:**
-   Ten Commandments (Exodus 20)
-   The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6)
-   The Beatitudes (Matthew 5)
-   Psalm 23, Psalm 1, Psalm 100
-   Romans 8:1, 8:28, 8:38-39

**Summary:** Systematic review prevents "learning and losing," building a permanent library of Scripture in the heart.

## 5. Psalmody and Hymnody

Singing is a command and a powerful aid to memory and emotion.

-   **Psalms:** We prioritize singing the Psalms (God's hymnal).
-   **Hymns:** We select robust hymns (e.g., "Holy, Holy, Holy," "A Mighty Fortress") over ephemeral songs.
-   **Folk Songs:** We also encourage folk songs for cultural literacy and joy.

**Practice:** Sing one Psalm/Hymn per month until mastered (all verses).

**Summary:** We fill the home with the sound of praise, connecting the child to the church of all ages.

## 6. Obedience Training

For younger children, obedience is the "grammar" of the Christian life.

-   **Principle:** "First time, all the way, with a happy heart."
-   **Method:**
    -   Give clear instruction.
    -   Expect compliance.
    -   Correct immediately if disobeyed (using parents' preferred discipline model consistent with Scripture).
    -   Restore fellowship immediately.
-   **Goal:** Self-discipline and submission to God's authority.

**Summary:** We train the will to submit to rightful authority as a picture of submission to Christ.
