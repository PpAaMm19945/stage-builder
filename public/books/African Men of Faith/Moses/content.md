# Dedication

To every sinner who thinks they are too far gone, proving that God's grace can reach anyone.

---

# Page 1
![Page 1](images/Page 1.png)

Long ago, in the hot sands of Egypt, there lived a man named Moses. Moses was not like other men. He was a giant! He was taller than a doorframe and stronger than an ox. But Moses did not use his strength to help people.

Moses had a very hard heart. He was the leader of a gang of robbers. Everyone was afraid of him. When he walked into a village, people would run and hide, whispering, "Here comes Moses! Run away!" He was lost in his sin, and he did not know God.

---

# Page 2
![Page 2](images/Page 2.png)

Because Moses did bad things, the law was chasing him. He had to run away. He ran far, far away into the Scetis Desert.

The desert was quiet. There were no riches there to steal. There were only monks—men who lived in caves and simple huts, praying to God. Moses thought he was just hiding from the police, but God had a different plan. God was about to catch Moses.

---

# Page 3
![Page 3](images/Page 3.png)

Moses expected the monks to be afraid of him, just like everyone else. He was a robber! He was scary!

But the monks were not afraid. They looked at this giant man with kindness. They gave him water and food. They treated him like a brother. Moses was confused. "Why are you nice to me?" he wondered. "I am a bad man." But the monks knew that God loves to save sinners.

---

# Page 4
![Page 4](images/Page 4.png)

Moses stayed in the desert. He watched the monks pray. He saw their peace. Slowly, the hard stone of Moses's heart began to crack.

He realized that even though he was strong, he was weak against sin. He cried out to God, "I have done terrible things! Can you really forgive a man like me?"

God answered. He didn't answer with thunder, but with a new heart. God chose Moses, not because Moses was good, but because God is gracious.

---

# Page 5
![Page 5](images/Page 5.png)

Moses became a monk. But changing his life was hard! He used to be wild and loud; now he had to be quiet and obedient.

Sometimes, he wanted to be angry again. Sometimes, he remembered his old life as a robber and missed it. This is called "the struggle." But Moses learned that he didn't have to fight alone anymore. He prayed, "Lord, give me strength to be gentle."

---

# Page 6
![Page 6](images/Page 6.png)

One day, Moses was sitting in his cell when four men kicked open the door! They were robbers from his old gang! They didn't recognize Moses because he looked so peaceful.

They pulled out their knives to attack him. In the old days, Moses would have fought them. He would have hurt them. The old anger rose up in his chest. What would he do?

---

# Page 7
![Page 7](images/Page 7.png)

Moses remembered that he was a new creation. He did not use his fists to hurt them. Instead, he used his great strength to stop them without hurting them at all!

He grabbed the four robbers—two in one arm and two in the other! He tied them up as easily as if they were bundles of straw. He was still Moses the Strong, but now he was Moses the Gentle.

---

# Page 8
![Page 8](images/Page 8.png)

Moses carried the robbers to the church and dropped them in front of the other monks.

"What should we do with them?" the monks asked.

Moses said, "I have no right to hurt anyone. God forgave me when I was a robber. We must forgive them, too."

The robbers were shocked. They recognized him now! "This is Moses the Terror!" they whispered. "But he... he let us go?"

---

# Page 9
![Page 9](images/Page 9.png)

Years went by. Moses became very wise. One day, the monks were angry at a brother who had made a mistake. They wanted to judge him. They called for Moses to come and judge the man, too.

Moses came, but he brought a strange thing with him. He carried a heavy basket filled with sand on his back. But the basket had a hole in it! Sand was spilling out everywhere as he walked.

---

# Page 10
![Page 10](images/Page 10.png)

"Father Moses, what are you doing?" the monks asked.

Moses said, "My sins run out behind me like this sand, and I do not even see them. Yet, I have come today to judge another man's sins."

The monks felt ashamed. They realized that they were all sinners saved by grace. Just like Moses, they needed to look at their own hearts before judging others.

---

# Page 11
![Page 11](images/Page 11.png)

The man who used to be a gang leader became a great leader of the church. They called him "Abba Moses." People traveled from far away just to hear his wisdom.

He taught them that real strength isn't about how much you can lift or how scary you can be. Real strength is fighting against the sin in your own heart and loving others.

---

# Page 12
![Page 12](images/Page 12.png)

Moses the Black showed the world that nobody is too bad for God to save.

If God can turn a violent robber into a gentle saint, He can change anyone. God takes our stony hearts and gives us hearts of flesh. Moses was the chief of sinners, but God's grace was stronger than his sin.

---

# A Note for Parents & Teachers

The story of Moses the Black is a powerful testimony of God's grace. Here are some themes to discuss:

- **Total Depravity:** Moses was a violent robber with a hard heart. This shows us that without God, our hearts are inclined toward sin and we cannot fix ourselves.
- **Unconditional Election:** God did not choose Moses because he was a good person. He chose him while he was still a sinner. God's love depends on His goodness, not ours.
- **Sanctification & Humility:** The story of the basket of sand teaches us about sanctification. Even after we are saved, we must focus on fighting our own sin rather than judging the sins of others. True strength is shown in humility and forgiveness.
