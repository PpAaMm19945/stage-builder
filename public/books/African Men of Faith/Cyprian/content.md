# Page 2
![Page 2](images/Page 2.png)

In the grand city of Carthage, where the sun always shone, lived a rich man named Cyprian.

He had the finest silks, a massive house, and piles of gold coins.

Cyprian believed that happiness was measured by how many valuable things he owned.

---

# Page 3
![Page 3](images/Page 3.png)

But one day, Cyprian heard about something far more precious than gold: the free, powerful grace of God.

He learned that God had forgiven all his sins through Jesus Christ.

When this truth filled his heart, the gold he owned suddenly looked dull. He felt a fierce, new love for God and for all people.

---

# Page 4
![Page 4](images/Page 4.png)

Cyprian traded his old life for his new faith. He sold his huge house and all his expensive things.

He gave all the money away, sharing it with the poor and the struggling.

He chose a simple life so he could serve God with everything he had.

---

# Page 5
![Page 5](images/Page 5.png)

Because of his deep faith and generous heart, the people of Carthage chose Cyprian to be their **Bishop**.

He became their shepherd, guiding the church with wisdom. He taught them: 'Because God has been so merciful to you, you must be merciful to everyone else!'

---

# Page 6
![Page 6](images/Page 6.png)

Then, a terrible disaster struck. A plague—a mysterious, deadly illness—hit Carthage like a storm.

People got sick fast. They coughed, shivered, and many sadly died. The whole city was overcome by panic and fear.

---

# Page 7
![Page 7](images/Page 7.png)

Everyone was terrified! Rich people ran away to the countryside. Doctors locked their doors.

Fear was so powerful that neighbors abandoned neighbors, and even family members ran away from their sick loved ones.

The poor and sick were left alone to die in the dusty streets.

---

# Page 8
![Page 8](images/Page 8.png)

But Bishop Cyprian did not run. He gathered his church and spoke with a strong voice:

"Friends, this is our time to shine! If we are in Christ, we do not fear death. Let us show the world the boundless love of God by serving those who need us most!"

---

# Page 9
![Page 9](images/Page 9.png)

Cyprian quickly organized the church into a great team of mercy. He divided the city into zones.

He matched the strong to the hard work, the wealthy to donating funds, and everyone to offering their time and care.

They set out to serve the sick with fearless love.

---

# Page 10
![Page 10](images/Page 10.png)

The Christians served **everyone**—not just their friends.

They cared for the neighbors who had ignored them and the pagans who had made fun of their faith.

They saw every suffering person as a precious neighbor in need, just as Jesus taught.

---

# Page 11
![Page 11](images/Page 11.png)

They worked all day and through the night. They brought fresh water to the thirsty and warm blankets to the shivering.

They cleaned the sick and, most difficult of all, they buried the dead with dignity and prayer.

In the dark city, the Christians were the only light of hope.

---

# Page 12
![Page 12](images/Page 12.png)

The non-believers were astonished. They asked the Christians, "Why do you risk your lives? Why do you help those who hate you?"

They had never seen such fearless kindness before.

---

# Page 13
![Page 13](images/Page 13.png)

Cyprian's people answered: "We do this because Jesus Christ first loved us.

God showed us the greatest kindness when we were His enemies. Now, His love moves our hearts to do the same for all people."

"This," they declared, "is what true faith looks like in action."

Because of the love, courage, and mercy of Bishop Cyprian and the church, many people saw the power of God's grace.

# A Note for Parents & Teachers

The life of Cyprian beautifully illustrates the practical outworking of Christian faith. Here are some themes to discuss:

- **Transformation and Justification:** Cyprian's decision to sell his riches and serve the poor (Page 4) demonstrates that true salvation results in radical life change. When we are forgiven (justified), our hearts are transformed, and our priorities shift from self-interest to serving others.
- **Radical Christian Mercy (Love for Neighbor):** The Christians cared for everyone, even those who hated them (Pages 10-11). This is the highest expression of Christian love, imitating Christ, who loved us while we were His enemies (Romans 5:8).
- **Faith Over Fear (Sovereignty):** Cyprian taught the believers that they had nothing to fear from death because their future was secure in Christ (Page 8). This encourages us to trust God's sovereignty over life and death, allowing us to serve others without being paralyzed by fear.
