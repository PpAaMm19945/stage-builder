# Dedication

To the children of the Reformation, who stand on the shoulders of giants.

---

# Page 1
![Page 1](images/Page 1.png)

Long ago, under the hot sun of North Africa, lived a boy named Augustine.

Augustine was smart. Very smart.

He could win any argument. He could memorize long poems. He could use words like a sword to poke and prod.

But Augustine had a problem. His heart was restless.

---

# Page 2
![Page 2](images/Page 2.png)

You see, Augustine didn't love God. He thought God's rules were boring.

One night, he and his friends crept into a neighbor's orchard. They found a tree heavy with pears.

They shook the branches until the fruit fell—Thump! Thump! Thump!

They didn't steal the pears because they were hungry. They didn't even like pears! They threw them to the pigs.

---

# Page 3
![Page 3](images/Page 3.png)

Augustine stole them just because it was wrong.

Sin is like that. It whispers that breaking the rules will make us free.

But it didn't make Augustine free. It made him empty.

He later wrote, "I loved my own error." He was stuck, and he couldn't get himself out.

---

# Page 4
![Page 4](images/Page 4.png)

Augustine had a mother named Monica.

Monica loved Jesus. She knew her son was running away from God, so she did the only thing she could do. She got on her knees.

Every day, she prayed. Every night, she wept.

"Lord, save my son!"

---

# Page 5
![Page 5](images/Page 5.png)

Augustine grew up and ran even further. He ran across the sea to Rome. He ran to the city of Milan.

He became a famous teacher. He wore fancy robes and gave speeches to Emperors. People clapped and cheered for him.

"Augustine is the smartest man in the world!" they said.

But inside, the smart man was miserable. He tried everything to fill his restless heart—friends, money, fame. But nothing fit.

---

# Page 6
![Page 6](images/Page 6.png)

God was hunting Augustine.

Not with a bow and arrow, but with kindness.

Augustine met a preacher named Ambrose who opened the Bible. Augustine listened. He realized that the Bible wasn't a boring book for simple people. It was the Word of the Living God.

Augustine wanted to change. He wanted to be good. But he couldn't stop sinning. He felt like he was in chains.

---

# Page 7
![Page 7](images/Page 7.png)

One afternoon, in a garden in Milan, Augustine fell to the ground under a fig tree. He was crying.

"How long, O Lord?" he wept. "Why can't I stop sinning? Why not now?"

Suddenly, he heard a voice. It sounded like a child singing a game from over the garden wall.

"Tolle lege! Tolle lege!"

---

# Page 8
![Page 8](images/Page 8.png)

It meant: "Take up and read! Take up and read!"

Augustine sat up. Was God speaking to him?

He saw a scroll of the Bible lying on the bench. He grabbed it. He opened it. His eyes fell on the book of Romans.

---

# Page 9
![Page 9](images/Page 9.png)

He read the words of the Apostle Paul:

"Not in wild parties and drunkenness... but put on the Lord Jesus Christ."

BAM!

It was like a light flooded into his heart. The chains fell off. Augustine realized he didn't have to try harder to be good. He needed to put on Jesus, like a new set of clothes.

---

# Page 10
![Page 10](images/Page 10.png)

Augustine didn't run anymore. He went home to Africa.

He became a Bishop. He spent the rest of his life writing books to tell the world one thing:

Grace.

He wrote that we are all like the boy in the pear tree—sinners who can't stop. But God is the Great Savior who chases us down.

---

# Page 11
![Page 11](images/Page 11.png)

Augustine died, and hundreds of years passed.

His books sat on library shelves. Dust covered them.

The Church began to forget what Augustine taught. They started to think they could buy God's love, or earn it by doing good things. They forgot about Grace.

---

# Page 12
![Page 12](images/Page 12.png)

But then, over a thousand years later, a German monk named Martin Luther found Augustine's books.

Martin was just like Augustine. He was trying hard to be good, but he felt like a sinner.

Martin read Augustine. And Augustine pointed Martin to the Bible. And the Bible pointed Martin to Jesus.

---

# Page 13
![Page 13](images/Page 13.png)

The light came on for Martin, just like it did for Augustine!

"It is by Grace alone!" Martin shouted. "Through Faith alone! In Christ alone!"

The Reformation began. The dusty books were opened. The Bible was preached again. The good news of Grace rang out like a church bell across the world.

---

# Page 14
![Page 14](images/Page 14.png)

Today, we read the same Bible that Martin read, and that Augustine read.

We learn that we don't have to run away. We don't have to earn God's love.

We just have to stop running, and let God find us.

---

# A Note for Parents & Teachers

This book is designed to be a conversation starter. Here are some key theological themes from the life of Augustine to discuss with your children:

- **The Pear Tree & Total Depravity:** Augustine taught that he sinned not for the fruit, but for the sin itself. This teaches us that our hearts are naturally turned away from God (Jeremiah 17:9). We need new hearts, not just better behavior.
- **The Garden & Irresistible Grace:** Augustine tried to fix himself for years and failed. It was only when God's Word (Romans 13) pierced his heart that he was changed. Salvation is Monergistic—it is the work of God alone.
- **The Reformation Connection:** The Reformers didn't invent their theology. They rediscovered the Bible, often with the help of Augustine. Augustine is often called the 'Grandfather of the Reformation' because he fought for the truth that Grace is a free gift.
